Privacy Policy — EISOL Calendar Sync

Last updated: 2026-11-23

This policy explains what information EISOL Calendar Sync ("the app") handles and how, including the Google user data it accesses. The app is made by EISOL LLC (英智ソリューションズ合同会社), 2-5-1 Sekimae, Musashino-shi, Tokyo 180-0014, Japan ("EISOL", "we").

What the app does

The app shows the Jira issues that a Jira administrator selects, by project or JQL, as all-day events in Google Calendar. A Jira administrator connects one Google account to the Jira site. The app makes one calendar for each rule in that account and keeps its events in step with Jira. If the administrator turns on "Change Jira from Google" for a calendar, moving an event in Google changes the issue's dates in Jira.

Where the app runs

The app runs entirely on Atlassian Forge, Atlassian's platform for apps. EISOL runs no servers for the app. During routine sync, issue details move from Jira to the connected Google account, and moved dates move from Google to Jira when that option is on, through Forge. The app does not send that content to a separate EISOL-operated server. Marketplace reporting, shared operational logs and support correspondence are separate information flows described below.

Information from Jira

  • Issue details written to Google. For each issue that matches a rule, has a due date and has no issue security level: the issue key, the summary (up to 250 characters), the status, the due date, the start date if the rule names a start date field, and a link to the issue. The app reads each issue's security level only to leave such issues out. It finds the issues by running the rule's JQL in Jira with its own access.
  • Site details. Your Jira site's web address, used in the links to issues and in each calendar's description, and the names of the site's custom date fields, for choosing a start date field.
  • Settings. The rules the administrator sets: a calendar name, the JQL or project key, the start date field, whether "Change Jira from Google" is on and since when, and the ID of the rule's calendar. The JQL is stored as written, including anything typed into it, such as an Atlassian account ID. The app keeps no Atlassian account IDs of its own.

Google user data

What the app accesses.

  • From the Google sign-in: the connected account's Google account ID, its email address and whether Google has verified that address, and the list of permissions that were granted.
  • The OAuth access and refresh tokens that Google issues to the app.
  • The calendars the app makes, and the events in them. To find changes, the app reads the events in those calendars, including any event someone adds to them by hand. It uses only each event's ID, version, status, dates, time of last change and the app's own private marker, which names the Jira issue and rule the event belongs to. Google's answers also contain the events' other fields, such as their titles, descriptions and the email address of the person who created them; the app does not use or keep those.
  • The app has no access to your other calendars or events, your contacts or your email.

How the app uses it.

  • The email address is shown on the app's settings page in Jira and on the page that shows the confirmation code, so that administrators can see which account is connected. The account ID keeps the Jira site tied to that account.
  • The tokens let the app call Google Calendar when Jira reports a change, when an administrator acts on the settings page, and every five minutes, without anyone present.
  • The calendars and events are used to create, change and delete the events for your issues, to put Jira's values back when an event is changed in Google, and, only for calendars with "Change Jira from Google" on, to write a moved event's dates to the issue's due date and start date in Jira.

What the app stores. In Forge hosted storage: the account ID (not its email address: the settings page reads the address from Google when it opens, and a new connection that waits for its confirmation code holds it for at most ten minutes), the granted permissions, and when the account was connected or disconnected; the tokens, in Forge's encrypted secret storage; each rule's calendar ID; for each event, the event's ID and version, the issue's key, the dates the event shows and a fingerprint (a short one-way hash) of what the app last wrote; and Google's change position for each calendar (a sync token and, during a long read, a page token). The app does not store event titles or descriptions, or anything from events added by hand.

Who it is shared with. Moved dates go to your Jira site, only for calendars with "Change Jira from Google" on. The email address is visible to the Jira administrators of your site. Atlassian hosts the app and stores this data on Forge. The app does not send Google user data to EISOL or to anyone else, and the log lines that Atlassian shares with EISOL do not include it, apart from Google's error codes.

What we never do. We do not sell Google user data, use it for advertising, use it to determine credit-worthiness or for lending, or transfer it to data brokers or information resellers. We do not use information received from Google APIs, including Google Workspace APIs, to develop, improve or train generalised or non-personalised AI or machine-learning models, and we do not transfer it to anyone for that purpose. People at EISOL do not read it, unless you send it to us yourself in a support request and ask us to look at it, or reading it is necessary for security, for example to investigate abuse, or to comply with the law.

Limited Use. EISOL Calendar Sync's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The use of information received from Google Workspace APIs will also adhere to the Google Workspace user data and developer policy.

Other information the app keeps

  • Recent changes. The last 20 things the app did: the time, the calendar name, the issue key and what happened, for example "event created" or "dates changed from Google (due 2026-12-08)". The settings page shows them to Jira administrators.
  • Error messages. Short descriptions of failed steps. The app keeps the last 30, and its settings page shows the five most recent to Jira administrators. They name the calendar and the issue key. When Jira refuses a change, the message includes up to 300 characters of Jira's answer, which can repeat part of the refused value. Google errors are reduced to the call, the status and Google's error code. These messages stay in the app's storage; the log lines Atlassian shares with EISOL never include Jira's answer.

Marketplace and support information. For paid apps, Atlassian makes license and transaction reports available to EISOL. They can include license or entitlement IDs, status, tier and dates; the customer organisation, site and country; and billing or technical contacts (name, email and, where provided, postal address and phone). EISOL accesses these reports from Japan for licensing and service administration. We also receive correspondence and files you choose to send us for support. These records are separate from the synced content.

The app does not collect card details, use cookies or track people. Atlassian handles Marketplace payments.

Where it is stored

The connection details, tokens, rules, links, change positions, recent changes and errors are kept in Forge hosted storage. Atlassian's Forge location documentation lists Australia, Canada, Germany and Ireland (EU), India, Japan, Singapore, South Korea, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States, plus a Global option whose realms Atlassian determines. Forge-hosted storage follows the host Jira site's pinned location and migration where applicable. Forge execution can sometimes occur in another location; storage pinning is not a promise that every processing step stays in that location.

The calendars and events, with the issue details the app writes to them, are stored in the connected Google account. Google's privacy policy says that Google maintains servers around the world and that information may be processed on servers located outside the country where you live. For a Google Workspace account, your organisation's agreement with Google also applies. These are provider-wide facts, not a confirmation of where your account's data is kept.

This app does not provide an app-wide residency or PINNED guarantee: the Google account and the GitHub-hosted connection page are outside Forge's storage controls. Marketplace reports remain on Atlassian's reporting service and are accessible to EISOL in Japan.

EISOL's own records. EISOL keeps support email with its email providers: SAKURA internet Inc. in Japan, and Google LLC (Gmail), which stores email in data centres in several countries, including the United States. Requests raised in the EISOL support portal are kept in Atlassian Jira Service Management on EISOL's Atlassian cloud site, which Atlassian stores in data centres in several countries, including the United States. To read messages in English and to draft replies, EISOL gives the text needed for a reply to AI services (currently Anthropic, PBC and OpenAI OpCo, LLC), with settings under which they do not use it to train their models; they may process it in the United States and other countries. EISOL accesses Marketplace reports on Atlassian's reporting service from Japan and keeps the copies it needs for its accounts on its own computers in Japan.

Who can see it

  • Your Jira users, according to Jira's permissions. The Jira administrators of your site also see, on the app's settings page, the connected Google account's email address, the rules, the recent changes and the recent problems.
  • People with access to the app's calendars in Google. The connected account's owner decides who they are. They see the issue details in the events, whether or not they can open those issues in Jira.
  • Atlassian, as the operator of Forge, under Atlassian's own terms and privacy policy.
  • Google, as the operator of the connected account, under the terms that apply to that account and Google's privacy policy.
  • EISOL develops and operates the app and is responsible for its processing of personal data under this policy; Atlassian is not responsible for EISOL's processing. The current app has no support feature for sending stored data or Google user data to EISOL. This is not a claim that EISOL receives no customer information: we can see Marketplace reports, support messages and the shared operational logs described here. When the app is installed, Atlassian shares the app's logs with EISOL by default, so that we can find faults; a site administrator can turn this off at admin.atlassian.com. The app writes no issue details and no Google account details to its logs, only what was involved (the calendar name and the issue key) and the kind of error, including Google's error code.

Connecting to Google

When an administrator connects Google, Google sends the browser to a page EISOL hosts on GitHub Pages (https://connect.eisol.jp/google-calendar/), with a one-time authorization code and connection state in the page's address. The page removes them from its address and forwards them to the app on Atlassian's platform; its own code does not persist them. The authorization code can be used only once, and only together with the app's client secret, which is not on that page and is never sent to GitHub. The app then keeps the approval waiting, and connects the account only when a Jira administrator of your site enters the confirmation code shown after the approval. This does not determine the hosting provider's logging or retention.

GitHub Pages documents that it logs and stores visitors' IP addresses for security, whether or not they are signed in. GitHub's default US storage description for GitHub.com is general: it does not establish that every Pages delivery copy or access log is stored only in the US. Its privacy statement also describes storage and processing in other countries. The full countries of storage for this Pages site's logs, whether and how its code/state query values are logged, and their fixed retention period remain unconfirmed. EISOL cannot see or change these logs.

How long it is kept

  • The connection details, rules, links and change positions are kept while the Google account is connected, and after a disconnect, so that the same account can be connected again. Recent changes keep the last 20 entries and error messages the last 30. An approval that waits for its confirmation code is deleted after 10 minutes.
  • When an administrator selects Disconnect, the app asks Google to revoke the site's access and deletes the tokens. Use another account then deletes the rest: the account ID, the rules, links, change positions, recent changes and error messages. Deleting a rule deletes its calendar in Google with all its events; the rule's links are deleted in the next runs.
  • After actual uninstallation from Jira, Atlassian manages the app's stored data under its Forge storage lifecycle. Atlassian currently documents a 28-day retention/recovery window; reinstallation alone does not restore the old data. This does not promise immediate erasure of all backups. Deletion through the app removes primary access; Atlassian's retention rules also apply to soft-deleted copies. Uninstalling does not revoke the app's access to Google: select Disconnect first.
  • The calendars and events stay in the Google account until they are deleted there, or until their rule is deleted while the account is connected. Dates the app wrote to Jira issues stay in Jira. Disconnect and Use another account do not delete these. Marketplace and support records are separate from Forge installation data; uninstalling the app does not erase them.
  • You can remove the app's access at any time in your Google Account, under Security → Your connections to third-party apps & services (myaccount.google.com/connections).
  • EISOL deletes support correspondence, including requests in the support portal, three years after the last message in a conversation. It keeps Marketplace reports and its other accounting records for seven years, the period Japanese tax law generally requires, and then deletes them.

Data protection roles

For personal data in the synced content and in the connected Google account's data that the app handles, the customer (the organisation that uses Jira and connects the Google account) decides what is synced and is the controller, and EISOL is the customer's processor for the processing the app performs. The app performs that processing on Forge and through the customer's Jira site and Google account. Operational logs, Marketplace reports and support correspondence are handled as described above. Our Data Processing Addendum forms part of the app's end-user agreement.

Your rights

To ask about the information the app handles, or to exercise your rights under data protection laws, contact us. Requests about synced content can usually be met in your Jira site and Google account, for example by deleting an event, a calendar or an issue. To end the app's access to a Google account, an administrator can select Disconnect, and the account's owner can remove the app in the Google Account settings. Contact EISOL about Marketplace contact records or correspondence held by EISOL.

Security

The app connects to Google with OAuth and never sees Google passwords. It asks Google only for access to the calendars it makes, plus the account's email address and ID. Tokens are kept in Forge's encrypted secret storage, the app's client secret is kept in an encrypted Forge variable, and all traffic uses HTTPS. Only Jira administrators can change the app's settings, and a connection starts only when an administrator of the site enters the confirmation code. Issues with an issue security level are never written to Google.

Changes to this policy

We publish changes on this page and update the date above. If a change materially affects how the app handles information, we also say so in the app's listing. Before the app uses Google user data in a new way or for a new purpose, we will update this policy, say so in the app's listing, and ask an administrator to agree to the change, for example by connecting Google again.
