EISOL Calendar Sync — User guide

EISOL Calendar Sync shows Jira issues with due dates as all-day events in Google Calendar. For each rule, which is a Jira project or a JQL query, the app makes a calendar in the Google account you connect and keeps its events in step with Jira. If you choose, moving an event in Google Calendar changes the issue's due date. The app runs entirely on Atlassian Forge.

See pricing.

Why the app asks for access to Google

When you connect a Google account, the app asks Google for three permissions. It uses them only for what this page describes:

  • The account's email address and Google account ID (openid and email). The settings page shows the email address, so that Jira administrators can see which account is connected, and the app keeps this Jira site tied to that account.
  • "Make secondary Google calendars, and see, create, change, and delete events on them" (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.app.created). The app makes its own calendars, one for each rule, and creates, changes and deletes the events in them. With this permission it cannot see or change your other calendars or their events.

The app writes Jira issue details to its calendars and, only if you turn this on, writes dates moved in Google back to Jira. It does not sell Google data, use it for advertising, or use it to train AI models. See section 10 and the privacy policy.

Before you start

  • You need to be a Jira administrator to set the app up.
  • You need a Google account that has Google Calendar: a personal Google account or a Google Workspace account. Accounts without Google Calendar, such as accounts made only for Google Cloud, cannot be used.
  • The app makes its calendars in that account, and only the account's owner can share them. Choose an account that your team will keep using.
  • One Google account is connected to one Jira site.

1. Install the app

A Jira administrator installs EISOL Calendar Sync from its Atlassian Marketplace listing, or in Jira under Apps → Explore more apps. The app's settings page is then under Settings → Apps → EISOL Calendar Sync. You can try the app free of charge before you buy.

2. Connect Google

  1. In Jira, go to Settings → Apps → EISOL Calendar Sync.
  2. Read Before you connect, which says what the app asks Google for and what it does with it, and select Connect Google. A Google page opens in a new tab.
  3. Choose the Google account to connect, check the permissions the app asks for, and allow them. If Google shows a box for each permission, keep the calendar permission ticked: the app cannot work without it.
  4. The page that opens shows a confirmation code and the Jira site it is for. Return to the app's settings page on that site, enter the code under Enter the confirmation code within 10 minutes, and select Finish connecting. The settings page then shows Connected to and the account's email address.

Nothing is connected until the code is entered. The code ties the approval in Google to an administrator of this Jira site, so a connection link that was forwarded, or sent to you by someone else, cannot connect an account by itself. If a colleague approves in Google for you, ask them for the code on their page. Enter a code only on the settings page of the site named on that page, and never give a code to someone who asks for it.

Google asks for consent each time you connect. To connect the same account again later, select Reconnect, or Connect again after a disconnect.

3. Add a calendar

Each rule makes one calendar. Select Add a calendar, then fill in:

  1. Calendar name: 1 to 50 characters. The calendar is called "Jira – " followed by this name. Each name must be different.
  2. Project key or JQL: a project key such as APT, which the app turns into project = APT, or any JQL up to 1,000 characters. Jira checks the JQL when you save. An ORDER BY is dropped.
  3. Start date field (optional): a date field such as Jira's Start date. An event then runs from the issue's start date to its due date. If the start date is empty or after the due date, the event shows the due date only. Keep None (the due date only) to use due dates only.
  4. Change Jira from Google: off by default. When it is on, moving an event in Google changes the issue's dates (section 6). Read the warning on the page before you turn it on.
  5. Select Make the calendar. The app makes the calendar in the connected Google account at once, and starts filling it within about five minutes. The Progress column shows how many issues it has gone over. Select Go over the issues now to start at once.

You can have up to five calendars. To change a rule, select Edit. A new name renames the calendar in Google, and new issues or a new start date field make the app go over the issues again. To delete a rule, select Delete and then Delete it and its calendar. This deletes the calendar in Google with all its events (section 9).

4. Share the calendar with your team

The app makes each calendar in the connected Google account, where it appears under My calendars. The app cannot share calendars, because the Google permission it uses does not allow it. The account's owner shares them in Google Calendar:

  1. Open Google Calendar on the web, signed in as the connected account.
  2. Point to the calendar "Jira – name", open its options menu and select Settings and sharing.
  3. Under Share with specific people or groups, select Add people and groups, enter your teammates or a group, choose See all event details, and send.

Everyone you share a calendar with sees the title (issue key and summary), status, link and dates of every issue in it, including issues they cannot open in Jira. The app leaves out issues with an issue security level, but it does not check who may see a project, so choose the issues with the calendar's audience in mind. Do not make the calendar public. Give Make changes to events only to people who may change the issues' dates, and only if you use Change Jira from Google. On Google Workspace, your administrator's settings decide whether calendars can be shared outside your organisation.

5. What syncs and when

  • Which issues: issues that match the rule's project or JQL, have a due date and have no issue security level. The app searches Jira with its own access, so a calendar can include issues that the administrator who set it up cannot see.
  • The event: an all-day event titled "[KEY] Summary" (summaries longer than 250 characters are cut), from the start date, if the rule uses one, to the due date. Its description holds the issue's status, a link to the issue and the line "Made by EISOL Calendar Sync from Jira." The event shows as free, so it does not block anyone's time.
  • Jira → Google: when an issue is created, changed or deleted, its event is created, changed or removed, normally within seconds. When the due date is cleared, the issue gets a security level, or it stops matching the rule, its event is removed.
  • Every five minutes: the app reads what changed in its calendars (section 6).
  • Once a day: the app goes over each calendar's issues again. It first removes events of issues that no longer belong, including issues the app can no longer see (deleted, or out of its reach), and then adds events that are missing, for example after a Jira change it missed. It also goes over them again at once after a rule changes, after the account is connected again, and after a pause of half an hour or more in its runs (for example while the license had lapsed), because Jira's changes of that time were not handled.
  • After each change it writes, the app reads the issue once more, so an issue that got a security level at the same moment loses its event at once.
  • Last run and Recent changes on the settings page show what the app did.
  • Not synced: other issue fields (such as the assignee, description and comments), attachments, issues without a due date, and times of day.

6. Changes made in Google Calendar

Jira is the source. Every five minutes, the app reads the changes made in its calendars:

  • If someone changes an event's title, description or dates, the app puts Jira's values back.
  • If someone deletes an event, the app makes it again while the issue has its due date.
  • An event that someone adds to the app's calendar by hand stays as it is. It is deleted with the calendar if the rule is deleted.

Change Jira from Google. When this is on for a calendar, moving an event changes the issue's due date, and its start date if the rule uses a start date field. The title and description still come from Jira. Only moves made after you turn it on are applied. An event changed to a time of day becomes an all-day event again, on its new date. If Jira refuses the new dates, for example because the field is not on the issue's edit screen, the event gets Jira's dates back and the problem is listed under Recent problems.

The app changes the dates with its own Jira rights, and Google does not tell the app who moved an event. So anyone who can change events in the calendar can change these issues' dates, even without the right to edit the issues in Jira. Share the calendar with Make changes to events only with people who may change the dates.

7. Limits

  • Up to five calendars on a Jira site, and one Google account for each site.
  • The Google account must have Google Calendar (section 8).
  • Calendar names have 1 to 50 characters, and JQL has up to 1,000 characters.
  • Start dates come from custom date fields, such as Jira's Start date. Date-and-time fields cannot be chosen.
  • Each run starts no new work after about 200 seconds, so a calendar with many issues is filled over several runs. Google's limits on how many events an account can create in a short time can slow this further.
  • The app reads up to 10,000 changed events from a calendar in one run. If Google asks the app to read a larger calendar again in full, changes made in Google to that calendar are not applied. Jira's changes still reach it.
  • Changes made in Google are read every five minutes, not at once.
  • Connect each Google account to one Jira site. Removing the app's access in a Google Account ends it for every site that uses the account, and disconnecting one site may do the same.
  • The app does not share calendars or invite anyone (section 4).

9. Disconnect, delete data and uninstall

  • Disconnect on the settings page stops the sync, asks Google to revoke this site's access to the account, and deletes the app's Google tokens. If Google cannot be reached, the problem is listed and the tokens are still deleted. The calendars stay in Google, still shared as you set them, but they stop following Jira: an issue that gets a security level meanwhile keeps its event until the sync runs again. The rules and links are kept, so connecting the same account again with Connect again continues with the same calendars, and the app goes over them again at once.
  • The same holds while the app's license is not active: the calendars stay as they are and stop following Jira until the license is renewed.
  • After a disconnect, Use another account and then Forget and continue make the app forget the Google account, its calendars, the links, the recent changes and the problems. The calendars themselves stay in Google. Delete them there if you no longer need them.
  • Delete on a calendar deletes the rule, and deletes the calendar in Google with all its events, including events people added to it by hand. The Google account must be connected.
  • You can also remove the app's access in your Google Account, under Security → Your connections to third-party apps & services (myaccount.google.com/connections). The sync then stops until an administrator selects Reconnect.
  • To stop using the app: delete the calendars you no longer need (with Delete, or in Google Calendar), select Disconnect, and then uninstall the app from Jira. Uninstalling alone does not revoke the app's access to Google, and leaves the calendars in Google, still shared, as they were. Atlassian deletes the app's stored data, tokens included, after its retention period.
  • Due dates that the app changed in Jira stay as they are.
  • If a connection is refused or never confirmed with its code, the app does not keep its tokens, but Google may still list the app under your connections. Remove it there if you like.
  • Change the connection from one administrator session at a time. Overlapping disconnect, reconnect or reset operations can require connecting again.

10. Data and privacy

The app runs on Atlassian Forge, and EISOL runs no servers for it. Issue details go from Jira to your Google account only, and dates moved in Google go to Jira only when you turn on Change Jira from Google.

  • What the app uses from Google: the connected account's email address and ID; and, in the calendars it made, each event's ID, version, status, dates, time of last change and the app's own marker, to find changes. Other event fields that Google returns, such as titles, are not used or kept. The app cannot see your other calendars.
  • What it writes to Google: the calendars "Jira – name", whose description includes your Jira site's address, and the events for your issues.
  • What it keeps in Forge: the account's email address and ID, the Google tokens (in Forge's encrypted secret storage), your rules, and, for each event, the event's ID and version, the issue's key, the dates the event shows and a fingerprint of what the app last wrote. It keeps no issue summaries and no event contents.
  • Who sees the account's email address: the Jira administrators of your site, on the settings page.

EISOL Calendar Sync's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

See the privacy policy, the Data Processing Addendum and support and service levels.
