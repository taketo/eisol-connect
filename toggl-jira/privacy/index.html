Privacy Policy — EISOL Time Sync

Last updated: 2026-11-22

This policy explains what information EISOL Time Sync ("the app") handles and how. The app is made by EISOL LLC (英智ソリューションズ合同会社), 2-5-1 Sekimae, Musashino-shi, Tokyo 180-0014, Japan ("EISOL", "we").

What the app does

The app copies time entries from a Toggl workspace into worklogs on a Jira Cloud site. A Jira administrator connects one Toggl workspace with the API key of a Toggl admin, and links Toggl people to Jira users. Each worklog is written in the name of the Jira user whose Toggl time it is, and only after that user has confirmed the link. The app reads from Toggl and writes nothing to Toggl.

Where the app runs

The app runs entirely on Atlassian Forge, Atlassian's platform for apps. EISOL runs no servers for the app. During routine copying, time entries are copied from Toggl through Forge into Jira. The app does not send them to a separate EISOL-operated server. Marketplace reporting, shared operational logs and support correspondence are separate information flows described below.

Information the app handles

  • Time entries read from Toggl. The app reads the workspace's entries changed since its last look; the entries of the last seven days, when it checks for deleted entries; the entries of the last 30 days, when an administrator looks for people; and a person's entries of up to the last 31 days, when they confirm or choose an issue. For each entry: the entry's ID, the Toggl user's ID and name, the start time, the duration, the description, the project's ID and name, and when the entry was last changed. The app writes the start time, the duration and the description (up to 1,000 characters, as the worklog's comment) to Jira. It does not keep descriptions: to notice a change, it keeps a fingerprint (a short one-way hash) of what it wrote.
  • People. For each Toggl person found when an administrator looks for people: their Toggl user ID and name. For each person linked to a Jira user: that user's Atlassian account ID, when the link was made, and when the user confirmed it. The app does not keep names or email addresses of Jira users.
  • Links between entries and worklogs. For each copied entry: the Toggl entry ID, the Jira issue ID and key, the worklog ID, the Atlassian account ID and Toggl user ID of the person, the entry's start time, a fingerprint of what was written, and when. For an entry Jira refused: a fingerprint and the time of the refusal. While an entry seems deleted: which check last missed it, and when Jira last refused to delete its worklog. For each day, the IDs of the entries copied for it, so deleted entries can be found.
  • Unassigned entries and choices. For confirmed people, their stopped entries of the last 31 days that have no issue: the entry ID, start time and length in minutes, not the description. These expire after 31 days. When a person chooses an issue for an entry, the app keeps the issue key: while the choice waits (31 days at most), and after the copy for as long as it follows that entry.
  • Recent copies. The last 30 copies: when, the Atlassian account ID and Toggl name of the person, the issue key, the minutes and what happened. Jira administrators see them on the settings page, and each person sees their own.
  • Connection details. The Toggl API key, kept in Forge's encrypted secret storage; the workspace ID; the Toggl user ID of the key's owner; when the workspace was connected; and the day copying starts.
  • Settings. Project rules: a Toggl project's ID and name, and an issue key.
  • Error messages. Short descriptions of failed steps. The app keeps the last 30, and its settings page shows the five most recent to Jira administrators. They name entries and issues by ID and key. When Jira refuses a worklog, the message includes part of the reason Jira gave (up to 300 characters). These messages stay in the app's storage; the log lines Atlassian shares with EISOL never include Jira's reasons.
  • Atlassian account IDs. As Atlassian requires of apps that store personal data, the app reports the Atlassian account IDs it keeps to Atlassian once a week. When Atlassian reports an account as closed, the app erases that ID wherever it keeps it: the person's link, the links of their copied entries, their choices and their recent copies. The worklogs stay in Jira.

Marketplace and support information. For paid apps, Atlassian makes license and transaction reports available to EISOL. They can include license or entitlement IDs, status, tier and dates; the customer organisation, site and country; and billing or technical contacts (name, email and, where provided, postal address and phone). EISOL accesses these reports from Japan for licensing and service administration. We also receive correspondence and files you choose to send us for support. These records are separate from the copied time.

The app does not collect card details, use cookies or track people. Atlassian handles Marketplace payments.

Where it is stored

The links, people, connection details, settings and errors are kept in Forge hosted storage. Atlassian's Forge location documentation lists Australia, Canada, Germany and Ireland (EU), India, Japan, Singapore, South Korea, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States, plus a Global option whose realms Atlassian determines. Forge-hosted storage follows the host Jira site's pinned location and migration where applicable. Forge execution can sometimes occur in another location; storage pinning is not a promise that every processing step stays in that location.

Copied time is also stored as Jira worklogs, in Jira's hosting location. Your time entries stay in your Toggl workspace, where Toggl keeps them under your agreement with Toggl; the app does not move or delete them.

EISOL's own records. EISOL keeps support email with its email providers: SAKURA internet Inc. in Japan, and Google LLC (Gmail), which stores email in data centres in several countries, including the United States. Requests raised in the support portal are kept in Atlassian Jira Service Management on EISOL's Atlassian cloud site, which Atlassian stores in data centres in several countries, including the United States. To read messages in English and to draft replies, EISOL gives the text needed for a reply to AI services (currently Anthropic, PBC and OpenAI OpCo, LLC), with settings under which they do not use it to train their models; they may process it in the United States and other countries. EISOL accesses Marketplace reports on Atlassian's reporting service from Japan and keeps the copies it needs for its accounts on its own computers in Japan.

Who can see it

  • Your Jira users, according to the permissions in Jira. A worklog's comment is the Toggl entry's description, and everyone who can see the issue's work log can read it.
  • Your Toggl users, according to the permissions in Toggl.
  • Atlassian, as the operator of Forge, under Atlassian's own terms and privacy policy.
  • Toggl, as the operator of your Toggl workspace, under your agreement with Toggl and Toggl's privacy policy. The app sends Toggl only the API key and its questions (which workspace, which people and which days).
  • EISOL develops and operates the app and is responsible for its processing of personal data under this policy; Atlassian is not responsible for EISOL's processing. The current app has no support feature for sending stored records or time entries to EISOL. This is not a claim that EISOL receives no customer information: we can see Marketplace reports, support messages and the shared operational logs described here. When the app is installed, Atlassian shares the app's logs with EISOL by default, so that we can find faults; a site administrator can turn this off at admin.atlassian.com. The app writes no time entries to its logs, only what was involved (entry IDs, issue keys) and the kind of error, and once a week the number of account IDs reported to Atlassian.

Connecting to Toggl

A Jira administrator pastes the API key of a Toggl admin on the app's settings page. The app checks it with Toggl, keeps it in Forge's encrypted secret storage, and sends it only to Toggl's API (focus.toggl.com), in the request header. It never shows the key again, and never writes it to logs or error messages. The key works with the rights of its Toggl user: the app uses it only to read the workspace's time entries, and the user's own account details and permissions.

How long it is kept

  • Links, people and settings are kept while the workspace is connected, so that changes and deletions in Toggl can be followed. The links of deleted entries are removed with their worklogs.
  • Unassigned entries and waiting choices expire after 31 days; a choice that was copied is kept with the entry's link. The app keeps the last 30 recent copies and the last 30 error messages.
  • Disconnect deletes the API key. The people, rules and links stay, so a key of the same workspace goes on from where the app stopped.
  • Reset the app deletes the key, the people, the rules, the links, the choices, the recent copies and the error messages.
  • After actual uninstallation from Jira, Atlassian manages the app's stored data under its Forge storage lifecycle. Atlassian currently documents a 28-day retention/recovery window; reinstallation alone does not restore the old data. This does not promise immediate erasure of all backups.
  • Worklogs already written to Jira stay there until you delete them, also after a reset or uninstallation. Marketplace and support records are separate from Forge installation data; uninstalling the app does not erase them.
  • EISOL deletes support correspondence, including requests in the support portal, three years after the last message in a conversation. It keeps Marketplace reports and its other accounting records for seven years, the period Japanese tax law generally requires, and then deletes them.

Data protection roles

For personal data in the copied time, the customer (the organisation that uses Jira and Toggl) decides what is copied and is the controller, and EISOL is the customer's processor for the processing the app performs. The app performs that processing on Forge and through the customer's Jira site and Toggl workspace. Operational logs, Marketplace reports and support correspondence are handled as described above. Our Data Processing Addendum forms part of the app's end-user agreement.

Your rights

To ask about the information the app handles, or to exercise your rights under data protection laws, contact us. Requests about copied time can usually be met in your Jira site and Toggl workspace, for example by deleting a worklog or an entry. A person can stop the copying of their own time on their page in the app. Contact EISOL about Marketplace contact records or correspondence held by EISOL.

Security

The Toggl API key is kept in encrypted storage, and all traffic uses HTTPS. Only Jira administrators can connect Toggl, link people or change rules. Time is written in a person's name only after that person confirms the link on their own page, and only while they do. A person can choose issues only for their own entries, and only issues they can log work on. The app changes and deletes only worklogs it created.
