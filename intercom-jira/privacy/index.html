Privacy Policy — EISOL Support Sync

Last updated: 2026-11-28

This policy explains what information EISOL Support Sync ("the app") handles and how, including the Intercom data it accesses. The app is made by EISOL LLC (英智ソリューションズ合同会社), 2-5-1 Sekimae, Musashino-shi, Tokyo 180-0014, Japan ("EISOL", "we").

What the app does

The app connects a Jira Cloud site to an Intercom workspace. A Jira administrator connects the workspace and chooses a Jira project. Support teammates create a Jira issue from an Intercom conversation with the data connector "Create Jira issue", and project administrators can link more conversations to an issue. The app copies new messages of linked conversations to their issue as comments, and writes the issue's comments and status changes to the conversations as internal notes. When an issue is done, it can put its closed or snoozed conversations back in the inbox.

Where the app runs

The app runs entirely on Atlassian Forge, Atlassian's platform for apps. EISOL runs no servers for the app. Conversation content moves from Intercom to the Jira site, and Jira comments and status changes move from Jira to Intercom, through Forge. The app does not send that content to a separate EISOL-operated server. Marketplace reporting, shared operational logs and support correspondence are separate information flows described below.

Information from Jira

  • Issue details written to Intercom. For each linked conversation, the app writes internal notes with: the issue key and a link to the issue (or to the comment); the text of each Jira comment that is not restricted to a role or group and was not written by an app, with its author's display name and time; each status change of the issue (the old and new status names); and, when a project administrator links a conversation by hand, the issue's summary. Settings choose which of these are written.
  • Site details. Your Jira site's web address, used in the links to issues.
  • Settings. The project for new issues and its issue type, the Intercom region, and the sync settings.
  • The app reads issues, comments and statuses with its own access, and checks the access of the person using the panel next to an issue. It keeps the IDs and keys of linked issues and the IDs of comments and status changes already written to Intercom, but no Atlassian account IDs.

Intercom data

What the app accesses.

  • At connection: the workspace's ID, name and region, from Intercom's answer to the app's "who am I" request. When the app writes a note, it asks again for the ID of the teammate the connection belongs to, and keeps that ID only in memory while it runs. The answer also contains the teammate's name and email address; the app does not use or keep them.
  • The OAuth access token that Intercom issues to the app.
  • When a teammate runs "Create Jira issue": the conversation's title and first message. Intercom's answer contains the whole conversation; the app uses only these.
  • Every five minutes: the list of conversations changed in the workspace. Intercom's answer contains each conversation's details, such as its first message and its customer. For conversations that are not linked, the app uses only their ID and update time.
  • For linked conversations: their messages, notes and replies (the newest 500 of each conversation), with each one's author name and type, time, number of attachments and whether it was written by this app; and, for the panel next to an issue, their title, first message, starting customer's name, state, update time and the IDs of their customers.
  • The data connector "Create Jira issue" that the app creates in the workspace, and the conversation ID that Intercom sends when a teammate runs it.
  • The app does not read contacts' profiles, companies, articles or your other Intercom data, and it does not send messages to your customers.

How the app uses it.

  • The workspace's name is shown on the app's settings page in Jira, so that administrators can see which workspace is connected. The workspace's ID keeps the Jira site tied to that workspace, the region chooses Intercom's address for the app's calls, and the teammate's ID is the author of the app's internal notes.
  • The token lets the app call Intercom when Jira reports a change, when a teammate runs the data connector, when someone uses the panel, and every five minutes, without anyone present.
  • Conversation content is written to Jira as described below, and is shown in the panel to people who can see the issue.

What the app writes to Jira. An issue created from a conversation gets a summary (the conversation's title, or the first line of its first message, or, when it has neither or Intercom cannot be read, "Intercom conversation" and its number), and a description with a link to the conversation and the first message (up to 2,000 characters). New messages of linked conversations become comments, each with its author's name, its kind (customer message, teammate reply or internal note), its time and a link to the conversation. Settings choose which kinds are copied; teammate replies are off by default. Attachments are not copied. Each issue the app creates and each comment it copies also carries a hidden Jira property with the conversation's ID (and, for a comment, the message's ID), which the app uses to avoid duplicates.

What the app stores. In Forge hosted storage: the workspace's ID, name and region, and when the workspace was connected or disconnected; the token and the data connector's secret, in Forge's encrypted secret storage; while an approval waits for its confirmation code (at most 10 minutes), the token, the workspace's ID, name and region and a hash of the code, also in the encrypted secret storage; the data connector's ID and state; the project, issue type, region and sync settings; for each linked conversation, the issue's ID and key, when it was linked, and the IDs and times of the newest messages already looked at; the time of the last look at Intercom; the package code that Intercom puts on the app's own notes; the IDs of Jira comments and status changes already written to each conversation; Jira changes still waiting to be written (IDs, the issue key, the status names and times); the last ten issues created from conversations (time and issue key); and the last 30 error messages. Apart from Jira's answers in error messages (below), the app does not store message text, the names or email addresses of customers or teammates, or teammates' IDs.

Who it is shared with. Conversation content goes to your Jira site. Jira comments and their authors' names, status changes, issue keys and links, and the summaries of issues that conversations are linked to by hand go to your Intercom workspace. When a teammate runs the data connector, Intercom also receives the issue's key and address. Atlassian hosts the app and stores its data on Forge. The app does not send Intercom data to EISOL or to anyone else, and the log lines that Atlassian shares with EISOL do not include message content or names.

What we never do. We do not sell Intercom data, use it for advertising, or use it to train AI models. People at EISOL do not read it, unless you send it to us yourself in a support request and ask us to look at it, or reading it is necessary for security, for example to investigate abuse, or to comply with the law.

Other information the app keeps

  • Recent issues. The last ten issues created from conversations: the time and the issue key. The settings page shows them to Jira administrators.
  • Error messages. Short descriptions of failed steps. The app keeps the last 30, and its settings page shows the five most recent to Jira administrators. They name the issue key, the conversation ID and, for a message, its ID. When Jira refuses a change, the message includes up to 300 characters of Jira's answer, which can repeat part of the refused value. Intercom errors are reduced to the call, the status and Intercom's error code. These messages stay in the app's storage; the log lines Atlassian shares with EISOL never include Jira's answer.

Marketplace and support information. For paid apps, Atlassian makes license and transaction reports available to EISOL. They can include license or entitlement IDs, status, tier and dates; the customer organisation, site and country; and billing or technical contacts (name, email and, where provided, postal address and phone). EISOL accesses these reports from Japan for licensing and service administration. We also receive correspondence and files you choose to send us for support. These records are separate from the synced content.

The app does not collect card details, use cookies or track people. Atlassian handles Marketplace payments.

Where it is stored

The connection details, token, settings, links, sync positions, waiting changes, recent issues and errors are kept in Forge hosted storage. Atlassian's Forge location documentation lists Australia, Canada, Germany and Ireland (EU), India, Japan, Singapore, South Korea, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States, plus a Global option whose realms Atlassian determines. Forge-hosted storage follows the host Jira site's pinned location and migration where applicable. Forge execution can sometimes occur in another location; storage pinning is not a promise that every processing step stays in that location.

Copies of Intercom content are stored in your Jira site, with your other Jira data. The notes and the data connector the app writes to Intercom are stored in your Intercom workspace, which Intercom hosts in the United States, the European Union or Australia, depending on the workspace (Intercom's regional data hosting). Your agreement with Intercom applies to that data.

This app does not provide an app-wide residency or PINNED guarantee: the Intercom workspace and the GitHub-hosted connection page are outside Forge's storage controls. Marketplace reports remain on Atlassian's reporting service and are accessible to EISOL in Japan.

EISOL's own records. EISOL keeps support email with its email providers: SAKURA internet Inc. in Japan, and Google LLC (Gmail), which stores email in data centres in several countries, including the United States. Requests raised in the EISOL support portal are kept in Atlassian Jira Service Management on EISOL's Atlassian cloud site, which Atlassian stores in data centres in several countries, including the United States. To read messages in English and to draft replies, EISOL gives the text needed for a reply to AI services (currently Anthropic, PBC and OpenAI OpCo, LLC), with settings under which they do not use it to train their models; they may process it in the United States and other countries. EISOL accesses Marketplace reports on Atlassian's reporting service from Japan and keeps the copies it needs for its accounts on its own computers in Japan.

Who can see it

  • Your Jira users, according to Jira's permissions. Everyone who can see an issue sees the comments copied from Intercom, including the names of the customers and teammates who wrote them and their messages, and, in the panel next to the issue, the linked conversations' titles or first lines, customers' names and states. The Jira administrators of your site also see, on the app's settings page, the connected workspace's name, the recent issues and the recent problems.
  • Your Intercom teammates, according to Intercom's permissions: they see the app's internal notes, which include Jira comments and their authors' names. Internal notes are not shown to your customers.
  • Atlassian, as the operator of Forge, under Atlassian's own terms and privacy policy.
  • Intercom, as the operator of your workspace, under your agreement with Intercom.
  • EISOL develops and operates the app and is responsible for its processing of personal data under this policy; Atlassian is not responsible for EISOL's processing. The current app has no support feature for sending stored data or Intercom data to EISOL. This is not a claim that EISOL receives no customer information: we can see Marketplace reports, support messages and the shared operational logs described here. When the app is installed, Atlassian shares the app's logs with EISOL by default, so that we can find faults; a site administrator can turn this off at admin.atlassian.com. The app writes no message content, names or email addresses to its logs, only what was involved (issue keys, conversation and message IDs), counts, and the kind of error, including Intercom's error code.

Connecting to Intercom

When an administrator connects Intercom, Intercom sends the browser to a page EISOL hosts on GitHub Pages (https://connect.eisol.jp/intercom/), with a one-time authorization code and connection state in the page's address. The page removes them from its address and forwards them to the app on Atlassian's platform; its own code does not persist them. The authorization code can be used only once, and only together with the app's client secret, which is not on that page and is never sent to GitHub. The app then keeps the approval waiting, and connects the workspace only when a Jira administrator of your site enters the confirmation code shown after the approval. This does not determine the hosting provider's logging or retention.

GitHub Pages documents that it logs and stores visitors' IP addresses for security, whether or not they are signed in. GitHub's default US storage description for GitHub.com is general: it does not establish that every Pages delivery copy or access log is stored only in the US. Its privacy statement also describes storage and processing in other countries. The full countries of storage for this Pages site's logs, whether and how its code/state query values are logged, and their fixed retention period remain unconfirmed. EISOL cannot see or change these logs.

How long it is kept

  • The connection details, settings, links and sync positions are kept while the workspace is connected, and after a disconnect, so that the same workspace can be connected again. Recent issues keep the last ten entries and error messages the last 30. A Jira change that could not be written to Intercom is tried again for a day and then dropped. If it cannot be tried in that time (for example while Intercom is disconnected), Forge deletes it automatically, two days after its last try. An approval that waits for its confirmation code expires after 10 minutes, and Forge then deletes it.
  • When an administrator selects Disconnect, the app deletes its Intercom token. The app does not revoke the token in Intercom, because the same workspace may be connected to other Jira sites, so the app's access to the workspace lasts until the app's token is revoked in Intercom (Settings → API tokens → Revoke). Use another account then deletes the rest: the workspace's details, the data connector's secret and record, the settings, links, sync positions, waiting changes, recent issues and error messages.
  • After actual uninstallation from Jira, Atlassian manages the app's stored data under its Forge storage lifecycle. Atlassian currently documents a 28-day retention/recovery window; reinstallation alone does not restore the old data. This does not promise immediate erasure of all backups. Deletion through the app removes primary access; Atlassian's retention rules also apply to soft-deleted copies. Uninstalling from Jira does not remove the app from Intercom: revoke its token there too (Settings → API tokens → Revoke).
  • Issues, comments and their copied content stay in Jira, and notes stay in Intercom, until they are deleted there. The data connector "Create Jira issue" stays in the Intercom workspace until it is deleted there. Disconnect, Use another account and uninstalling do not delete these. Marketplace and support records are separate from Forge installation data; uninstalling the app does not erase them.
  • EISOL deletes support correspondence, including requests in the support portal, three years after the last message in a conversation. It keeps Marketplace reports and its other accounting records for seven years, the period Japanese tax law generally requires, and then deletes them.

Data protection roles

For personal data in the synced content and in the Intercom data that the app handles, the customer (the organisation that uses Jira and connects the Intercom workspace) decides what is synced and is the controller, and EISOL is the customer's processor for the processing the app performs. The app performs that processing on Forge and through the customer's Jira site and Intercom workspace. Operational logs, Marketplace reports and support correspondence are handled as described above. Our Data Processing Addendum forms part of the app's end-user agreement.

Your rights

To ask about the information the app handles, or to exercise your rights under data protection laws, contact us. Requests about synced content can usually be met in your Jira site and Intercom workspace, for example by deleting a comment, a note or an issue. To end the app's access to an Intercom workspace, an administrator can select Disconnect in Jira and revoke the app's token in Intercom under Settings → API tokens. Contact EISOL about Marketplace contact records or correspondence held by EISOL.

Security

The app connects to Intercom with OAuth and never sees Intercom passwords. It asks Intercom only for the permissions it needs: reading and writing conversations, reading admins, and reading and writing data connectors. The token is kept in Forge's encrypted secret storage, the app's client secret is kept in an encrypted Forge variable, and all traffic uses HTTPS. The data connector calls the app with a random secret that the app sets in the connector and keeps in Forge's encrypted secret storage. The app creates the data connector for teammates only, with Fin's use turned off. It sends the app only the conversation's ID and returns only the issue's key and address. Customer authentication is off because nothing is returned to the customer, and Set it up again turns it off again. Only Jira administrators can change the app's settings, only project administrators can link or unlink conversations, and a connection starts only when an administrator of the site enters the confirmation code. Apart from reopening a conversation when its issue is done (if that setting is on), everything the app writes to conversations is an internal note, which your customers do not see.

Before the app uses Intercom data in a new way or for a new purpose, we will update this policy, say so in the app's listing, and ask an administrator to agree to the change, for example by connecting Intercom again.
